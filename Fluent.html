Fluent Conveyors  |  B2B SaaS  ·  Multi-tenant customer portal

Turning a support-heavy aftermarket into a self-serve platform.

Fluent sells heavy-duty conveyors, but earns for years afterwards on parts, service and maintenance, a revenue stream that ran entirely on phone calls and PDFs. I designed the multi-tenant SaaS portal that moves that whole aftermarket online: every facility gets its own workspace to manage assets, request and e-sign quotes, pay invoices, track shipments and stay ahead of maintenance, while Fluent's dealers stop being a human API.

01 The business case

The aftermarket was the business. Manual process was the tax on it.

A conveyor sells once and is serviced for a decade. Parts, service and maintenance are the durable revenue, and every dollar of it was moving through inboxes, phone calls and printed PDFs. That isn't only an experience problem, it caps how much aftermarket a fixed headcount can carry, and it hands the relationship to whoever answers the phone fastest.

Cost to serve

Dealers were the interface

Every quote, status check and reorder consumed a salesperson's day. Support load scaled linearly with customers.

Revenue leakage

Slow quotes lose the order

A line down and a day of quote latency is how an OEM loses a parts order to a third-party supplier.

Retention

No account of record

With asset and service history living in customer spreadsheets, nothing anchored the customer to Fluent between purchases.

Expansion

Nothing to upsell into

Preventive maintenance and service contracts need a system of record to be sold, tracked and renewed.

The design brief, stated as a business goal

Make the aftermarket self-serve: every routine transaction a customer completes without a phone call is margin recovered, a faster order, and one more reason the next one comes to Fluent.

The problem

Four manual loops, each with a cost attached.

Fluent engineers and manufactures heavy-duty conveyors for recycling and waste facilities, equipment that can't afford downtime. Once a machine shipped, everything after it ran on people. Research traced the friction to four loops, and each one billed the business in a different currency.

A

No system of record

Operators tracked assets, parts and service history in spreadsheets, or not at all.

Business cost: no account of record, so no basis for renewals, service contracts or retention.

B

Quote-to-cash ran on email and PDFs

Requesting parts, receiving a quote and signing it bounced between reps, printers and scanners.

Business cost: days of quote latency and orders lost to faster third-party suppliers.

C

Maintenance was reactive

Teams discovered a worn bearing only when a line went down, costing throughput.

Business cost: unplanned downtime for the customer, and no predictable parts demand for Fluent.

D

Status requests absorbed the sales team

"Where's my shipment?" meant another phone call to the dealer, who forwarded a tracking number.

Business cost: cost-to-serve rising with every new account, selling time spent on support.

Aftermarket & customer-success leadership at Fluent

Tenancy, roles & permissions

The structural work a customer never sees, and immediately feels when it's wrong.

A portal serving many facilities is a multi-tenant product before it is a set of screens. Two customers must never see each other's assets, pricing or service history, one customer with four sites must see all four, and a dealer must be able to act on behalf of an account without becoming an unlogged back door. I designed that model explicitly rather than letting it emerge from the screens.

Tenant

The customer account

Assets, quotes, invoices, pricing and history are scoped to the account. Multi-site customers get sites beneath one account rather than four disconnected logins.

Roles

Operator · account admin · dealer

Operators raise carts and tickets; account admins approve spend and e-sign; dealers quote and support. The same screen, three different sets of primary actions.

Approval

Who is allowed to commit money

The person who finds the part usually can't authorise the purchase. Signing and payment sit behind the admin role, so self-serve never means uncontrolled spend.

05 The platform

Quote-to-cash, anchored to the asset.

The portal is organized around a single mental model: your assets are the home base, and every action, parts, quotes, payment, service, flows from them. Following the sequence below, the commercial loop that used to take a week of email closes inside one product.

01 / Assets

Every line, at a glance.

My Assets lists every conveyor line with its health, status and next-service date, searchable and filterable by site, so an operator sees what needs attention before anything breaks.

  • ✓Health, status & next service per asset
  • ✓Search and filter by site or status

Everything about a machine, in one place.

Drill into any asset for its details, uptime and maintenance status, plus a full parts & components list, then add a part to the cart, raise a help-desk ticket, or open a quote, all scoped to that machine.

  • ✓Searchable parts catalog per asset
  • ✓Add to cart, raise a ticket or task in context

02 / Inventory

Every part, and its stock.

The Inventory page lists every part with its global stock and status, in stock, low, or out, searchable across the account.

  • ✓Global stock and status per part
  • ✓Search and filter by status

Manage stock, location by location.

Open a part to see the locations that hold it, then adjust stock at any location without leaving the flow.

  • ✓Stock levels per location
  • ✓Manage stock in context

03 / Cart → Quote

A cart that becomes a quote.

Because conveyor parts are made-to-order and freight varies, the cart doesn't check out, it sends a quote request to the dealer. The customer keeps a running estimate while the dealer firms up freight and lead time.

  • ✓Parts grouped by the asset they belong to
  • ✓Request routes straight to Open Quotes

04 / Open Quotes

Every quote, in one list.

The Open Quotes page lists every quotation with its status, value and valid-until date, so a customer sees what's new, in progress, pending sign-off, or cancelled at a glance.

  • ✓Status, value & validity per quote
  • ✓Search and filter by status or line

Read it, then sign it.

Open any quote for its full line-item breakdown, scoped to the asset, with bill-to, ship-to and totals, then download, print, or e-sign and accept in one place.

  • ✓Line items priced against the asset
  • ✓Download, print, or e-sign to accept

05 / Invoice → Pay

Every invoice, in one place.

The Invoices page lists every invoice across the account with its due date, payment method and status, searchable across assets.

  • ✓Due date, method & status per invoice
  • ✓Search and filter by status

Open it, then pay it.

Open any invoice for its full line-item breakdown, then pay by card or on purchase-order terms, download, or print, no phone call required.

  • ✓Pay by card or PO terms in-app
  • ✓Download or print the invoice

06 / Shipments

Pick an address to track.

Track shipments starts from a delivery address, each showing how many shipments are on the way, so a multi-site customer jumps straight to the one they need.

  • ✓Every delivery address in one list
  • ✓Shipment counts per address

Every shipment for that address.

See each shipment with its carrier, ship date and live status, in transit, delivered, or delayed, so nobody has to phone a dealer for an update.

  • ✓Carrier, ship date & status per shipment
  • ✓Search and filter by status

Follow it to the door.

Open any shipment for a progress timeline from order to delivery, the items inside, carrier and ETA, plus a full tracking history of every scan.

  • ✓Order → processing → in transit → delivered
  • ✓Items, carrier, ETA and scan history

07 / Maintenance

Every work order, by urgency.

The Maintenance page groups every work order by urgency, upcoming, due and late, so teams service equipment before it fails.

  • ✓Late / due / upcoming, at a glance
  • ✓Search and filter by asset or status

From checklist to done.

Open any work order for its task checklist, parts used, assigned technician and history, then complete it, request a quote, or raise a ticket, all logged back to the asset.

  • ✓Task checklist, parts and technician
  • ✓Complete a task → logs to asset history

08 / Help Desk

Every ticket, from raised to resolved.

Tickets can be raised from any asset and land on the Help Desk. Open one for the full conversation with support, its priority and assignee, and the related asset, part and work order, all in context.

  • ✓The full support thread, in one place
  • ✓Linked to the asset, part and work order

07 Test

Testing the path to first value, not the pixels.

Adoption was the whole risk: a portal customers abandon changes nothing, no matter how it looks. So the moderated sessions with operators and dealers ran against the activation path itself, find a part, get a quote, sign it, pay, track it, on a clickable prototype. Every issue below is one that would have sent a customer back to the phone.

Participants · operators & dealers

What testing changed

Severity What we observed What we changed
High

Cart → quote felt like a dead end

Users built a cart but weren't sure how to turn it into a quote request.

 Added a persistent "Request quote" summary panel with a clear primary action on the cart itself.
High

E-signature was missed

Several operators scrolled past the signature step and tried to place the order.

 Made signing an explicit, blocking step with an inline sign-and-accept affordance before order placement.
Medium

Maintenance status read as decorative

Due / late chips weren't perceived as actionable.

 Promoted overdue items to the dashboard with count badges and a direct link to schedule.
Medium

Part-to-asset link unclear

Users weren't certain a part fit their specific machine.

 Anchored the parts catalog inside the asset and labeled compatibility explicitly.
Medium

Shipment ETA hard to scan

Tracking timeline was understood but ETA took a beat to find.

 Surfaced the next milestone & ETA at the top of the tracking panel.
