SmartLearning  |  EdTech  ·  Learning management system for cybersecurity & data science

From a skills assessment to a cybersecurity career, in one platform.

SmartLearning trains people for careers in cybersecurity and data science, with live mentorship, industry certifications and career support at the end. This case study covers the platform I designed end to end: the learner journey from a personality and skills assessment through to a credential, the trainer tools that deliver it, and the admin and enterprise consoles that run it.

About the project

Online learning that ends in a job, not just a certificate.

SmartLearning uses a cohort and mentorship model: learners take a pre-course assessment, follow a personalised path through cybersecurity or data science, work with live mentors, and finish with a recognised certification and career support. The platform serves two markets at once, individuals building a new career and organisations upskilling whole teams, so the same product has to hold a learner, a trainer, an enterprise admin and the platform team.

Background

The security and data skills gap is real and growing, yet most online courses stop at the video. SmartLearning was built to close the loop between learning and employment: assess where a person is, guide them along a path with human mentorship, certify what they can actually do, and support the move into work. An online platform was the right vehicle because that loop has to run at scale, across individuals and enterprise teams, in one place.

Stage 01 · Empathize

Understanding the people.

I interviewed 12 career-switchers and early-career professionals to learn where existing platforms fall short, alongside 4 trainers who deliver courses and 3 enterprise learning leads who manage teams. The interviews were synthesised into an affinity map that surfaced the frustrations below.

Top frustrations uncovered

The gap was never content. It was a path.

Learners were not short of videos to watch. They were short of direction, feedback, and a credential that meant something to an employer.

Understanding trainers & enterprise users

The people who deliver and manage it.

Beyond learners, I sat with the people who run the training day to day. Two roles, two very different jobs for the platform to do.

Trainers

Deliver courses and mentor cohorts. They need the platform to get out of the way so they can teach and give feedback fast.

Content tools Progress tracking Live scheduling Fast grading

Enterprise admins

Manage whole teams and answer for the spend. They need a clear view of who is progressing, and simple control over seats and access.

Bulk enrollment Team reporting Seats & billing Permissions

Problem statement

People who want a cybersecurity or data career have no shortage of courses, but no guided path, no feedback, and no credential employers trust. They need a platform that assesses where they are, mentors them along the way, and proves what they can do at the end.

Product vision · focus areas

Skill development

Real, assessed skills, not passive video.

Guided pathways

A path matched to the learner's level and goal.

Live mentorship

Human feedback and 1-2-1 support built in.

Credential validation

Certification and career support that lands on a CV.

Results & takeaways

What shipped, and what it taught me.

SmartLearning shipped as one platform holding four roles, with the learner journey running from assessment to certification and career support. The biggest shift from the original plan was moving mentorship from a bolt-on to the spine of the experience, which is what makes the credential mean something. Next, I would deepen the enterprise reporting and the AI tutor.

Focus on the MVP

Scoping to the assessment-to-certificate loop kept the first release shippable and coherent.

Stay close to the problem

Writing each decision against a learner frustration kept the team user-focused.

Design for every role

One product holding four roles meant designing the seams, not just the screens.
