Luxury Marketplace  ·  B2B & B2C  ·  Web UI/UX

Where a Birkin changes hands without a leap of faith

Dossier is a luxury resale marketplace on the web, serving individual collectors and professional resellers, B2C and B2B, who buy, sell, and trade high-value bags, shoes, and accessories. Nobody clicks “add to cart” on a ₦25 million handbag, and in this market the fear has one name: fakes. The design problem is trust at every step, proving the piece is authentic, the seller is real, and the money is safe, without making the experience feel like an interrogation.

Approach

A user-centered process

Every decision in Dossier traces to something a person does, fears, or needs. The work put empathy before assumptions, choosing to sit with the fear of fakes before proposing a cure. It valued real feedback over guesswork, testing each flow against persona scripts rather than taste. It treated refinement as continuous rather than a final polish, and it held accessibility as a floor the design stands on, not a feature added at the end.

What I did

With no live user base to interview, I used the honest equivalents: market research on the resale category, weeks reading buyer and seller community forums, a study of the resale horror stories people share, a competitive audit of the existing luxury resale platforms, and synthetic persona sessions run against early flows.

Personas

The burned buyer

B2C · high-value collector

“I have been fooled once. I will not spend this kind of money on a maybe.”

Has paid 5 figures before and been handed a fake, or watched it happen to someone close. Wants certainty far more than a bargain.

Gives up when proof starts to feel like marketing.

The individual seller

B2C · one grail piece

“It is real, it is mine, and I can prove none of it.”

Has one grail piece to move and a clean history, but no instrument to prove either. Wants a fair price without feeling accused.

Gives up when listing feels like a customs interrogation.

The trade reseller

B2B · 30 pieces a month

“Give me proof I can move fast, or I sell somewhere else.”

Moves 30 pieces a month and lives on margin and speed. Wants the same proof standard to be the fast path, not the friction.

Gives up when the console fights their volume.

What the research found

  1. 01Buyers do not fear high prices, they fear fakes at high prices.
  2. 02Sellers with clean histories have no instrument to prove them.
  3. 03Both sides distrust the single moment the money moves.
  4. 04A badge that says verified, with nothing behind it, reads as marketing, not proof.
  5. 05Speed and certainty are treated as opposites, when resellers need both at once.

Empathy first, in practice

The insight that reframed the project was quiet: neither party actually wants to be trusted. The buyer does not want to extend faith, and the seller does not want to ask for it. What both want is to stop needing trust at all, to replace a leap of faith with a chain of evidence. From then on, every screen moved away from persuasion and toward proof.

02 · Define

Naming the problem

The problem statement

A buyer paying 5 figures for a pre-owned piece has no instrument for certainty except the seller’s word, and the seller has no way to offer more than it.

How might we

How might we replace a leap of faith with a chain of evidence, at every step of a high-value sale?

  • How might we let a seller prove a clean history without it reading as a sales pitch?
  • How might we make the moment money moves feel safe to both sides at once?
  • How might we give a reseller speed and a collector patience from one system?

The design principles

  • Evidence over assertion

    Nothing claims to be true without showing why, so every status links to what backs it.

  • The examiner decides

    Authenticity is settled by an independent expert, never by either party’s confidence, which removed self-verification from the flow entirely.

  • Ask only what the step needs

    Each screen requests the one detail it cannot proceed without, so proof never turns into an interrogation.

  • Name the consequence

    Every action states what it will do before it is taken, including the option to walk away.

  • The record outlives the sale

    Ownership and outcomes persist on the piece and on the seller, so history compounds instead of resetting with each listing.

Success criteria

  • A first-time buyer can explain why a piece is trustworthy without reading help text.
  • A seller can list a piece and see exactly what proof is required before price is set.
  • At the moment money moves, both sides can name what happens next and how to stop it.

03 · Design

Building the solution

From structure to screens

The build went flows first, then wireframes, then high fidelity. Three structural decisions carried the product. The listing became a dossier, a file an expert prepared, which answers the fear of fakes with evidence instead of a badge. The transaction became a staged, visible escrow tracker, which answers the fear of the moment money moves by making every stage legible. The seller gained a public record that carries outcomes, which answers the unprovable history by letting a clean past accumulate in the open.

One trust system, on every listing

Authentication statusOwnership historyEscrow state

Information architecture

27 screens across 3 access zones. The account you hold decides which zone opens; proof lives on the listing, and the seller record is written by the system, not the seller.

Persistent chrome, every page

Logo home · zone-aware tabs · help menu · notification bell (30 days, one per deal state change) · cart · theme toggle · account menu. Footer: authentication standard, escrow partner, consign, cookie preferences.

Reading the chart

Screen Gate System-written

Task flow · buying a piece

A buyer never ships, never inspects, and never pays a fee. What they commit to is an amount and a wait: the money sits with the escrow partner until the examiners clear the piece. Every ending is on the chart.

What the buyer is told

Price and escrow total, nothing else. The 8% commission and the $150 reading are the seller’s side of the ledger and never appear here.

Walking away

Free any time before acceptance. After acceptance, up until release, the hold is returned and nothing is charged.

Notification

One email and one bell entry per state change, kept 30 days. If Dossier emails, a deal moved.

Task flow · selling a piece

A seller ships once, at intake, and never again. Everything after that happens inside the vault, and every outcome is written to a record the seller cannot edit. Every ending is on the chart.

Tier changes limits, not rules

Individual and trade see the same screens and the same record. Trade unlocks bulk intake, desk tools and no cap on live lots. Upgrade in Settings.

Custody, in one line

Piece into the vault at stage 1, money into escrow at stage 2, pre-release check at stage 3, then out to the buyer and released to you.

What you cannot do

Edit a plate, hide a failed reading, or take payment outside escrow. The record is written by the system.

Lo-fi design

Greyscale wireframes locked the hierarchy before any polish: proof above price on the listing, the escrow state always in view, the seller console built for volume.

The listing, as a dossier

The final screen. The piece is presented like a file an expert prepared: price beside its market range, a chip reading you can replay yourself, and the whole audit a scroll away. Proof leads, the buy button waits.

The macro read · chip, stitch, hardware

Every claim carries a photograph behind it, written in the examiner’s own words: the chip payload decoded, the saddle stitch read at 60×, and the hardware weight matched to the blind stamp.

The escrow stage tracker

The money and the piece both pass through the marketplace, never party to party. Funds sit in escrow, owned by neither side, from the moment they are paid until an independent examiner verifies the piece. A pass releases the money to the seller and the piece to the buyer; a fail refunds the buyer.

The visual language

The aesthetic follows the object. Generous whitespace, near-black and warm ivory, and a single gold accent held back for verification, so the one color that signals proof is never diluted by decoration. Serif headlines carry the voice while a quiet grotesque carries the details, where legibility matters more than character. The interface stays calm because the stakes are high, and restrained because trust whispers.

Light and dark, one system

The same screen holds in two surfaces. Dark is the gallery, where the piece is lit against near-black and the interface recedes. Light is the ivory document, where the record reads like paper. Tokens carry the whole palette, so a single switch moves the ground, the type, and the lines together, while the gold stays reserved for verification and the hierarchy never changes. Both surfaces clear AA contrast.

Accessibility as a floor

Accessibility was a floor the layout had to clear, not a feature bolted to the end. Text meets AA contrast in both the dark experience and the ivory product surface, labels sit outside fields and never collapse into placeholders, and color is never the only signal, verification always pairs the gold with a word and a check. Every control is keyboard reachable and every state is announced. One real defect surfaced in review: the first pass set field labels at low opacity for elegance, which failed contrast, and they were restored to full weight.

04 · Test

Real reactions, real fixes

Before
After

The same listing, two fixes at once: the gold verified mark now waits for the examiner rather than sitting on the seller’s claim, and the record leads with price beside its market range, so the evidence is read before the cost.

How I tested

Testing used the methods available before launch: walkthroughs driven by persona scripts, synthetic user sessions against the prototype, hallway tests of the core flows, and a heuristic review of every state.

What broke, and what changed

  • The confirm-receipt button handed one party a weapon. A buyer could withhold confirmation to stall release and pressure the seller.

    The fix

    Release now triggers on the examiner’s verdict, not the buyer’s tap. Neither party controls the other’s money.

  • The verified badge appeared on the seller’s claim, before any examination. It read as marketing, the exact thing buyers distrust.

    The fix

    Verified now appears only after the expert stage, and that is the only place the gold is allowed to show.

  • The listing led with price. It framed the piece as a product, and the fear of fakes came straight back.

    The fix

    Price moved to sit beside provenance, never alone, so the evidence is read before the cost.

Continuous improvement

Iteration was continuous rather than a final polish. After launch, the flow would be measured where it can fail, offer completion, drop-off at funding, and time to first offer, then refined against those numbers.

The product proves that certainty can be designed, not promised. The process proved that the fastest way to earn trust is to make it unnecessary. In luxury, restraint is the brand, and the interface earns trust the way a good dealer does, by showing its work and never raising its voice.
